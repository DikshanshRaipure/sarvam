# Product Vision

## *The world as it is*

In the professional space where the local language is a mandate and not a choice, the inconvenience and the hardship of creating the documents in that particular language becomes a nightmare at the professions with a high volume of documentation jobs.

## *Why this is a technology gap, not a skill or willingness gap*

The professionals working with and putting up with the mandatory confinement of the language restrictions cannot use technology to mitigate this hardship, not because they cannot employ technology at all but because the current available transcription technology just doubles their work of proofreading and correcting what they tried transcribing.

The current transcription models were not trained specifically on the intricacies of the Indic  languages, or have been done with hardly any dedication.

## *Why now / why possible now*

Sarvam’s transcription model on the other hand has always been India first. Sarvam’s current speech-to-text model, Saaras v3  was trained on 1M+ hours of real Indian speech. Saaras v3 achieves 19.31% WER on IndicVoices across 10 languages and outperforms GPT-4o Transcribe, Gemini 3 Pro, Deepgram Nova-3 and Scribe v2 on Indian-language accuracy.

## *The mechanism, not just the outcome*

A  lawyer (who usually prefer to draft the complete case files themselves), sits down with his/her system and KIVI auto launches on startup, opens up his text editor and just presses down the “⌃” key to say “Civil Divorce Case Appeal Template”. The complete ready to edit template just snaps into existence without the lawyer browsing through the template documents and copy pasting it.  
Time saved.  
Now, the lawyer just goes to the part where he actually needs to. Add the context for the case and then again press the same “⌃” key to just speak all the draft into existence.

## *Boundaries*

As tempting as it sounds to stage Kivi app as a communication gap bridging technology for a grandmother living in village who cannot type to send messages to her grandson directly via speaking, I would really be cautious in not explicitly staging Kivi as that app, but rather an app which can do the both, but weighing more towards the professional end of the spectrum which I've been describing so far.

Although the grandma example had a more sentimental and moral value driving behind it, the professional example is where actually the scaling of Kivi app can happen.

## *What "worth keeping" means over a year, not a week*

The complete idea behind "worth keeping" for our app is to ensure that the user just cannot go back to typing again, which is actually the case. 

Once the user starts dictating and tries to revert back, the operational timing becomes such a massive pushback that it feels utterly unproductive to spend again an hour for drafting a single document typing in orthodox way when the same person was finessing more than 3 or 4 in the same timespan speaking into KIVI’s assistance.

For a competitive space as in the lawyers office as well, the speed might just be the dealbreaker. Slower client documentation might cost the lawyer the new clients whom the professional couldn’t cater to just because the per client consulting span was eating up the whole time.

A lawyer performing orthodoxically in the office and typing every client details will take an hour to draft the case appeal in the native language, whereas a professional lawyer using KIVI could easily speak 3 to 4 appeals into existence catering to 3 to 4 clients in that same hour. One is able to cash in about 10k in the Civil Divorce Appeal drafting while the other is scaling to nearly 40k in the same span. The difference speaks for itself.

KIVI makes their day-to-day operation scalable without which their productivity crumbles to dust.
